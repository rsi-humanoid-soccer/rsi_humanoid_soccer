HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP RECURSIVE SELF-IMPROVEMENT

Training Kick and Dribble Skills from a Single Reference Motion.

One reference motion. An LLM that plans every new version. A human who writes one sentence of feedback.

From a single generated reference, the loop trains a humanoid Kick over a range of ball positions and a Dribble that follows changes of direction, with no task-specific data collection.

OVERVIEW

One reference. One loop. One robot.

Training a kick from one reference motion usually ends at one ball position. We keep going. After the first kick policy the tracking term is dropped, and the LLM decides every later version from the failure statistics and one sentence of human feedback. The rollouts of the best policy, failures included, carry over to the next version; the weights do not.

01

Single reference

One generated reference motion starts the first version, shared by Kick and Dribble. No expert demonstration is collected for the task.

02

LLM planning

The LLM may change the task distribution, reward terms, domain randomization, hyperparameters and thresholds, and nothing about the evaluator.

03

Human feedback

A human watches the best checkpoint of each version and writes a sentence, for example which behavior is weak.

04

Data inheritance

The best policy's rollouts, failures included, fill part of every training batch of the next version, which starts from fresh weights.

DESIGN

What stays fixed, and what changes.

Fixed

The RL solver, the evaluator, the success criteria and the real-robot evaluation grid.

Changed by the LLM

The task distribution, reward terms, domain randomization, hyperparameters and thresholds, one version at a time.

The human

Watches the best checkpoint of each version, writes a sentence of feedback, and declares when to stop.
